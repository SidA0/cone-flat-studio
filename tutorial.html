02 / GEOMETRY

How a cone unfolds

Circle becomes arc. An original 3D circle radius r becomes a flat radius R = r / sin(α), where α is the cone half-angle.

One turn becomes a sector. The flat angle is the model’s circular sweep multiplied by sin(α). For a complete cone it is 360° × sin(α).

Slant width is preserved. The difference between the outer and inner flat radii is the true generator length across the sheet.

Body 2 · 18° cone

05 / SCOPE & CHECKS

Before ordering the cut

Supported STEP geometry

The reader supports analytic CONICAL_SURFACE faces bounded by two circular arcs and two straight generator edges. It joins matching half-faces from the same shell. Spline, faceted mesh, toroidal, cylindrical, or irregular trimmed faces require another CAD flattening workflow. SI metre-based STEP length units are converted to millimetres.

Fabrication inputs

There is no automatic kerf offset, seam overlap, weld gap, bend compensation, or nesting. The STEP is a zero-thickness planar sheet face, not a solid sheet. The pattern follows the detected outer conical surface. If you need a neutral-axis blank, set the offset in CAD or with your fabricator based on material and forming process.

Always compare at least one radius and overall flat dimension against the STEP model, inspect excluded faces, and confirm the seam and cut method with the shop. A model with multiple cone slopes or nondevelopable transitions may need separate blanks or a formed transition.
